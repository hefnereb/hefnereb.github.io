1. The first thing I learned is the importance of becoming familiar with AI tools that are actually being used in the field. Tools like Claude Code show how AI can support development workflows, from writing code to debugging and problem-solving. Even if you don’t rely on them heavily, understanding how they fit into real-world work is important and can give you an advantage going into the job market.
2. The second thing I learned is how interesting and unpredictable innovation can be, especially through looking at patents. Some of the patents we discussed were unusual, but they showed how creative problem-solving can take many different forms. It gave me a better understanding of how ideas turn into real-world technologies and how those ideas can make companies money. I also learned that some people get patents for the stupidest/strangest things. 
3. The third thing I learned is the value of staying engaged with current events and discussions. The daily “what’s going on” conversations helped me stay more aware of what’s happening in the country and around the world. It also made the class more engaging and showed how computing connects to real-world issues beyond just technical work

One piece of advice I would give to students taking this class next semester is to stay engaged. Try to participate in discussions and interact with both the professor and other students. It’s a much more relaxed environment and not very stressful to speak up. You get a lot more out of the class that way, and it makes the material more interesting and easier to connect with.
